# Patagonian-Canopy-Height-Models
The dataset comprises a Canopy Height Model (CHM) derived from UAV-LiDAR surveys of native Nothofagus forest and ponderosa pine (Pinus ponderosa) plantations in Patagonia. Data acquisition was conducted during the 2024 austral summer (leaf-on season) using a YellowScan® Surveyor Ultra LiDAR system equipped with a Hesai XT32M2X scanner and mounted on a DJI® Matrice 300 RTK quadcopter. Operating at a wavelength of 903 nm, the laser scanner emits 640,000 pulses per second and records up to three returns per pulse, with a system accuracy of approximately ±3 cm.
Survey flight parameters included a mean flying altitude of 70-100 m  above ground level (AGL), a flight speed between 3.5 and 5 〖"m s" 〗^(-1), and a field of view of 70^∘ - 120^∘. Data were acquired using single and double 45^∘ cross-grid flight patterns with 60"-" 92% swath overlap and 20"-" 70 "m"  flight line spacing, yielding an average point density of 152- 1174 〖"pts m" 〗^(-2). Trajectory post-processing was conducted in Applanix POSPac to generate Smooth Best Estimate Trajectory (SBET) files, incorporating base station data from a GNSS Spectra SP60 L1/L2 receiver set over a known geodetic benchmark. Point clouds were subsequently processed in YellowScan CloudStation for strip adjustment and ground classification. Digital Terrain Models (DTMs) and Digital Surface Models (DSMs) were generated at spatial resolutions of 0.25 "m"  for El Foyel and 0.5 "m"  for Manso Medio and La Zeta. Canopy Height Models (CHMs) were calculated as the algebraic difference between the DSM and DTM ("CHM"="DSM"-"DTM" ). Finally, data post-processing was completed in RStudio (version 2025.09.2+418) to reclassify height outliers exceeding 50 "m"  as NA and reproject the final CHM products to the WGS 84 geographic coordinate system (EPSG:4326).
